Practice

One method, five disciplines.

I work in Blender 5.1 and Python on pieces that sit between fields. A four-minute orchestral score written in mido becomes a 6,720-frame EEVEE render. A conversation with Natsume Sōseki becomes a WebSocket audio pipeline with a SQLite memory. A question about whether student loans actually matter becomes an agent-based model with bootstrap confidence intervals and a peer-reviewed PDF.

The aesthetic stays consistent even when the medium doesn't: dark grounds, emissive objects, cold light with a single warm rim. I don't build toward photorealistic daylight. I build toward the moment a structure becomes visible — a city made entirely of gears, the Milky Way seen from inside a room, a crystal you can see through but never quite see.

Method

Five steps, in this order, every time.

The order matters — it's the only thing that keeps a piece from becoming decoration. Each stage borrows its discipline's way of asking.

  1. 01Philosophy

    Frame the question

    Decide what is actually being asked before anything is modelled. If the question can be answered with a picture alone, it wasn't the question.

  2. 02Physics

    Build the mechanism

    Gears, orbits, light falloff, entropy, credit constraints. The structure has to hold together on its own before it is lit.

  3. 03Literature

    Give it a narrative

    A cut, a run, a phone call, a sentence. Time is the material that turns a mechanism into something a person can sit through.

  4. 04AI

    Let the machine explore

    Latent search, generated variation, simulated agents. The machine proposes far more than I would; I keep the few that survive.

  5. 05Aesthetics

    Composite, grade, cut to length

    Cold key, warm rim, near-black ground. Then the render budget and the viewport frame rate get the final vote.
